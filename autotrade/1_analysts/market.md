## SPY trend analysis

### 1) Primary trend: still strongly bullish
SPY has recovered aggressively from the March drawdown and is now trading near the upper end of its 6-month range. The sequence from the early-April base to mid/late-May is especially constructive:

- **Late March / early April**: sharp selloff into the low 630s, followed by stabilization.
- **April 8 onward**: a decisive reversal began, with a series of higher highs and higher lows.
- **Mid-May**: SPY accelerated into the 740s, showing that dip buyers remain active and that the market is willing to pay up on strength.

This is the behavior you would expect in a healthy uptrend: the index does not just recover, it expands to new highs with only shallow interruptions.

### 2) Short-term trend: strong, but somewhat extended
The most recent price action is bullish, but the market is no longer in the “early recovery” phase.

Recent candles show:
- **May 14**: strong push to **748.17**
- **May 15–19**: mild digestion/pullback
- **May 20–22**: rebound back toward **745.64**

That sequence suggests SPY is consolidating near highs rather than breaking down. Consolidation near the highs is generally constructive, but after a strong vertical move it can also mean momentum is becoming overextended and more vulnerable to mean reversion.

### 3) Volume profile: supportive, but not euphoric
The rebound from the April lows happened on solid participation, with several sessions showing elevated volume during the reversal and follow-through phase. More recently, though, the advance has been accompanied by somewhat more moderate volume than the panic-reversal days.

That matters because:
- strong rallies with expanding volume are more durable,
- rallies that continue with declining participation can still rise, but they become more sensitive to any macro headline or sector rotation.

So the tape is still constructive, but not “blow-off” bullish.

- short-term exhaustion,
- a likely pullback toward the low 720s,
- or at minimum a transition from trend acceleration to range behavior.

### Risk management
If trading long:
- Stops should likely be placed using **ATR-based distance** rather than arbitrary tight stops.
- Because SPY has recently been volatile, normal noise is likely larger than in a calm trend.
- A stop too tight risks getting shaken out even if the larger trend remains bullish.
